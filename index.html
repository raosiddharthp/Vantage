01 Client Brief

Bishopsgate General Insurance — the anchor client.

A composite of real UK mid-market commercial insurer patterns. Every requirement, constraint, and design decision in this document is grounded in the specific operational reality of a commercial lines insurer whose claims, underwriting, legal, customer service, and renewals functions run on five separate systems that were never built to talk to each other.

Bishopsgate General Insurance has spent 58 years underwriting commercial property, casualty, and marine risk for SME and mid-corporate clients across the UK, on the promise of "cover you can rely on when it matters." That promise is evidenced well at the point of sale — pricing is sharp, underwriting is disciplined — and evidenced poorly at the point of claim, where the average file takes over six weeks to close and no function outside claims handling sees what happened until long after it stops being useful to them. Chief Claims Officer Helen Marsh has funded two prior claims-technology initiatives that improved reporting without changing the underlying cycle time, and is the executive sponsor for a third attempt that has to show a different kind of result.

What the brief is, precisely
Not a claims-automation platform, and not a rebuild of ClaimCenter. Vantage is the layer that turns one FNOL submission into a single structured record that Claims, Legal, Underwriting, Customer Service, and Renewals can each read from the moment it exists — instead of each function independently, slowly, and partially reconstructing the same claim on its own timeline.

03 Problem Statement

Not a claims-handling failure. A single moment of information nobody else in the business gets to see.

Bishopsgate's claims handling is not badly run — Adeyemi's team is diligent and the file quality on audit is good. The failure is structural: the average claim now takes 44 days from first notice of loss to final payment, the longest since industry tracking began, and every minute of that delay compounds because manual intake creates bottlenecks, fragmented systems don't share data cleanly, and different channels deliver inconsistent service. Claims is not a cost centre Bishopsgate can shrink by cutting corners — claims handling is an insurer's single largest cost component, historically running to around 70% of premium collected — so a small percentage improvement in cycle time is a large absolute number, not a rounding error.

Failure 1 — Evidence gets buried, not lost
Everything Adeyemi needs to write his briefing already exists in the FNOL submission — photos, the incident description, a contractor's correspondence. The extraction work, not the judgment, is what eats his day.
Failure 2 — Downstream functions find out too late
Okonkwo learns about a subrogation opportunity from the closed file, by which point evidence has degraded and the window has narrowed. Chandra sees loss severity in the renewal review, months after the loss occurred, not the week it was scored.
Failure 3 — The policyholder experience is silent
Nobody proactively updates Elder & Vance, so every status question becomes an inbound call to Kim's team — and a slow, silent claim is consistently the largest single driver behind a subsequent decision not to renew.

A fourth thread runs above all three: the same claim is rediscovered five times, independently, at five different moments. Marsh's board reporting is built on whichever of those five moments happened to be visible when the report was pulled.

Precise problem statement
Bishopsgate has no mechanism that turns a single FNOL submission into a structured signal usable by every downstream function with a stake in it — so Claims, Legal, Underwriting, Customer Service, and Renewals each re-discover the same claim late, from a different angle, at a different time, at real cost to recovery, pricing accuracy, and retention.

04 As-Is Architecture

Five systems, one of which sees the claim first — and stops there.

The FNOL submission lands in ClaimCenter through whichever channel the policyholder or broker used, and it stays there. Underwriting, Legal, Customer Service, and Renewals each have their own systems, and none of them are wired to read anything out of ClaimCenter until a human manually pulls a report — typically at renewal, at claim closure, or when a complaint forces the question.

05 Cost of Cycle Time

Cycle time is not only a handling-cost metric. It is a retention lever.

The claims-cost case is well understood on its own: claims requiring manual review can take days or weeks and cost up to $20 each to process, and claims associates spend up to 80% more time on manual data entry than an automated process would require, which is time diverted from the risk assessment and evaluation work that is their actual expertise. Digitisation has a well-established ceiling on what it recovers: McKinsey research finds digitised claims processes can reduce handling costs by up to 30%.

The retention case is the one that changes who in the business should care. In an Accenture survey of digital insurer claims customers, 41% of claimants said they wanted to switch insurers, citing poor claims service as the reason. That figure sits directly upstream of Whitfield's renewal numbers, not just Marsh's claims budget.

The compounded annual cost of the current cycle time is not speculative. Manual extraction across 38,000 claims a year, subrogation opportunities found too late to fully pursue, and renewal attrition on the subset of accounts with a genuinely poor claims experience together represent a quantifiable floor of £1.8–2.4M per year — before the harder-to-quantify cost of Marsh's own credibility the next time she asks the board to fund a claims initiative.
Benchmark Sources & Methodology
Manual review cost and cycle time: industry claims-automation research on FNOL-to-payment duration and per-claim manual review cost.
Claims cost share of premium: Deloitte, cited in Five Sigma FNOL research, claims as ~70% of premium collected (2020 baseline).
Digitisation cost-reduction ceiling: McKinsey & Company research on digitised claims handling cost.

06 User Stories

Six stakeholders, six stories, one underlying record.

US-01 · Claims Handler
As a claims handler, I want the facts in a new FNOL submission already extracted and structured, so that I spend my time adjusting the claim rather than transcribing it.
Acceptance: every extracted field cites the source document and page; low-confidence fields are flagged, not silently filled in; the handler can accept, correct, or reject each field before it is treated as fact.
US-02 · Legal & Subrogation Counsel
As subrogation counsel, I want to be notified the moment a claim shows third-party involvement, so that I can preserve evidence and assess recovery while the window is still open.
Acceptance: the flag states the evidence that triggered it and nothing more — no liability determination is asserted; counsel makes the pursue/decline call.
US-03 · Underwriting Manager
As an underwriting manager, I want a severity signal against my book as soon as a claim is scored, so that renewal pricing reflects current loss experience rather than a stale annual snapshot.
Acceptance: the signal rolls up to account and portfolio level; it is additive to Chandra's existing feed, not a second source of truth she has to reconcile.
US-04 · Customer Service Lead
As a customer service lead, I want a plain-language status update generated automatically at key claim milestones, so that policyholders don't have to call to find out where things stand.
Acceptance: updates are reviewed against a confidence threshold before sending; anything uncertain routes to a human rather than going out wrong.
US-05 · Renewals & Distribution Director
As the renewals director, I want an account-level churn-risk flag ahead of the renewal date on accounts with a poor claims experience, so that I can re-engage before the broker moves the business.
Acceptance: the flag is delivered with enough claim-experience detail to act on, at least four weeks ahead of renewal date where the claim timeline allows it.
US-06 · Chief Claims Officer
As the executive sponsor, I want one dashboard tying cycle time, subrogation recovery, and claims-linked retention together, so that I can show the board a single, defensible outcome rather than five disconnected metrics.
Acceptance: every number on the dashboard is traceable back to the individual claims that produced it.

07 Proposed Design

One Claim Intelligence Record, generated once. Five role-based views read from it — nothing downstream is replaced.

The design deliberately resists the instinct to build a separate agent per stakeholder. A single pipeline processes the FNOL submission once and produces one structured Claim Intelligence Record. Every downstream stakeholder view — Claims, Legal, Underwriting, Customer Service, Renewals — is a permissioned read of the same record, not a separate system with its own copy of the truth.

Guardrail

Vantage drafts. It does not decide coverage, does not determine liability, and does not authorise payment. Every field in the Claim Intelligence Record is either extracted with a citation to its source document, or explicitly marked low-confidence and routed to a human before any downstream view is permitted to treat it as fact. This mirrors an existing regulatory boundary, not a design preference Bishopsgate could relax later — claims decisions are accountable acts under FCA ICOBS claims-handling rules, and Clarke's assurance role depends on that boundary being structural, not procedural.

Data access boundary

Each role reads only the fields its function is permitted to see. Okonkwo's Legal view surfaces liability evidence and subrogation flags; it does not surface Kim's customer-service sentiment notes. Whitfield's Renewals view surfaces an account-level churn-risk score; it does not surface the underlying claim file. The record is one object internally, but no view exposes more than its role needs — enforced at the query layer, not left to interface design.

10 Architecture Decision Records

ADR-VT-01Single record with role-based views, not a per-stakeholder agent
Context: Five functions each want a tailored output from the same claim event.
Decision: One pipeline produces one Claim Intelligence Record; every stakeholder view is a permissioned read of that record.
Rejected: A separate agent per function, evaluated and rejected — it multiplies extraction cost five times over for data that is identical at the source, and creates five points where the same fact could drift out of sync.
Status: Accepted.
ADR-VT-02Human-in-the-loop retained for every coverage, liability, and payment decision
Context: Claims handling decisions are accountable acts under FCA ICOBS.
Decision: Vantage drafts and cites evidence; it never finalises a coverage decision, a liability finding, or a payment. The handler or counsel of record makes every determination.
Consequence: Cycle-time gains come from removing manual extraction and cross-functional latency, not from removing decision points — bounds the upside, and is the correct trade for a regulated claims-handling function.
Status: Accepted.
ADR-VT-03Confidence-threshold routing, not best-effort auto-population
Context: Extracted fields vary in reliability — a clean invoice line item and an ambiguous liability inference are not equally trustworthy.
Decision: Fields below a set confidence threshold are marked and routed to a human reviewer rather than populated into the record as fact.
Consequence: Some claims move through the pipeline more slowly than others — by design; a wrong subrogation flag or a wrong customer update is a worse outcome than a slower one.
Status: Accepted.
ADR-VT-04Read-pattern integration with existing systems, no re-platforming
Context: Mehta's team has been burned by prior tools that promised to "just read" and became a maintenance burden.
Decision: Vantage reads from ClaimCenter, Duck Creek, and existing downstream systems through their existing APIs; it dispatches notifications into existing workflows rather than requiring any of the five systems to change.
Rejected: A shared data warehouse consolidating all five systems — rejected as disproportionate for the problem and a multi-year programme in its own right, not this project's scope.
Status: Accepted.
ADR-VT-05Least-privilege view boundary per stakeholder role
Context: The record contains fields with different sensitivity — customer sentiment, legal evidence, financial severity.
Decision: Access to each field is enforced at the query layer by role, not by interface design alone — a Renewals view cannot retrieve raw claim-file contents even if the interface were modified.
Consequence: Clarke's assurance function can verify the access boundary from configuration, not by auditing every interface.
Status: Accepted.

11 Objection Handling

Genuine trade-offs, addressed directly — not talking points to overcome.

Adeyemi — "is this a surveillance tool measuring me, dressed up as help?"
A real risk if the metrics attached to Vantage become individual handler scorecards. The design commitment is that Vantage's output is a draft the handler edits, not a benchmark the handler is measured against — that has to be an explicit management decision at rollout, not an assumption.
Okonkwo — "an AI-suggested subrogation flag is still legal exposure if it's wrong"
Correct, and why ADR-VT-02 exists. The flag states the evidence, not a conclusion — "third-party correspondence present, contractor named" rather than "contractor liable." Counsel's determination is the only thing Legal acts on.
Mehta — "another integration is another thing that breaks at 2am"
A legitimate operational cost. ADR-VT-04 keeps the integration surface to read access against existing APIs and notification dispatch into existing channels — smaller than a new system, but not zero, and on-call ownership needs to be assigned before rollout, not after the first incident.
Chandra — "I already have a claims feed; why is this not redundant"
A fair challenge. The distinction is latency, not data — her existing feed is accurate but arrives at renewal review; Vantage's severity signal arrives the week a claim is scored. If that latency gap doesn't matter for her pricing cycle, this view isn't worth building for her function.

13 Rollout Roadmap

Three phases. Each one working end to end before the next begins.

Phase 1Claims Handler view only, Commercial Property line. Document AI + Gemini pipeline, Claim Intelligence Record, handler briefing UI. Proves extraction accuracy and cycle-time impact on one line before any other view is built.
Phase 2Add Legal and Underwriting views on the same line of business. Subrogation flag and severity signal — the two views with the clearest, most defensible £ case.
Phase 3Add Customer Service and Renewals views; extend the pipeline to Casualty & Liability and Marine & Cargo lines.

Each phase is scoped to be independently defensible — if Phase 1 doesn't measurably reduce cycle time on Commercial Property, Phase 2 does not proceed on schedule. The roadmap is a sequence of proof points, not a committed multi-year programme sold on Phase 1's promise.
